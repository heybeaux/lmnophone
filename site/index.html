LMNOPhone GitHub

Take your attention back.

LMNOPhone (say it like the alphabet song: “elemeno-phone”) turns an ordinary Android phone into one that only opens the apps you chose. You decide what gets your attention, and someone you trust helps you keep it that way.

LMNOPhone is early and still being built. There’s nothing to download yet.

Who it’s for

People who want their attention back

Someone who picks up the phone for one thing and loses an hour, or who finds a smartphone tiring or confusing. They still need a few real apps: maps, music, the bank, the family chat. No tinkering needed.

The person who helps them

A partner, a grown-up child or a good friend. LMNOPhone calls them the trusted person. They sit down with the phone, together, to add an app or keep it up to date.

Or you, for yourself

You can look after your own phone, too. LMNOPhone adds waits and limits that make giving in to a tempting app harder. It can’t stop you, and it says so.

It isn’t built for watching someone. It’s built for deciding, together, what your phone is for.

What it looks like

Black, white and grey. Big words, no icons, nothing that asks for your attention.

  • Ask for an app. Say which app you’d like and what it’s for. Your trusted person gets a text.

  • A link that can’t open. General browsing is blocked. Some links can open approved apps, and some approved apps have web pages inside them. You can look at blocked links together later.

  • For the trusted person. Adding apps and keeping the phone working, behind a PIN. LMNOPhone doesn’t show them your messages or photos.

Design mockups using the app’s real wording. Margaret and Sam are example names.

The promise

Who holds the keys, and what they can and can’t do, in plain words.

  1. A person you trust holds the keys. Not a company.

    Changes happen on the phone itself, with your trusted person’s PIN. There’s no website where someone changes your phone from far away.

  2. App choices, not a window into your life.

    Your trusted person can add and remove apps. LMNOPhone doesn’t give them your messages, photos or a record of what you do in apps. Someone holding an unlocked phone can still see what’s on it.

  3. They stay with the phone while making changes.

    When opening Settings or checking for app updates, your trusted person stays with the phone until they tap Done and LMNOPhone confirms it is ready to hand back. If LMNOPhone stops running during that time, access may stay open until a helper fixes it.

  4. Big changes wait, so there’s time to say no.

    Changes like turning LMNOPhone off normally wait at least 24 hours with the phone on. Time while it’s off doesn’t count. While a change is waiting, anyone holding the phone can cancel. LMNOPhone tries to text each trusted person about the change. In supervised mode, a trusted person can use their recovery code to skip the wait.

  5. 9-1-1, calls and texts come first.

    LMNOPhone is designed to keep emergency calls, incoming calls, texts and emergency alerts available. Device or network failures can still interrupt them.

  6. No LMNOPhone account, server or tracking.

    LMNOPhone runs on the phone. Requests travel as ordinary text messages; your carrier’s usual charges may apply. LMNOPhone doesn’t send us your activity. Android and approved apps can still use their own accounts and collect data. LMNOPhone can’t stop that.

  7. Normal turn-off keeps your photos and messages.

    A successful turn-off restores an ordinary Android phone without erasing your photos or messages. A serious failure may need a factory reset; anything not backed up can be lost.

  8. Honest about the limits.

    Before anyone agrees to use it, LMNOPhone writes down, in plain words, what it can’t stop.

Help build it

LMNOPhone is open source and built in the open. Anyone can read the code, check what it does, and run it on their own phone. If you write Android code, design for people who find technology hard, test on older phones, or just have good ideas about phones that respect people’s attention, we’d love your help.
