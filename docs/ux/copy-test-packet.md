# Copy test packet (for a person who has never seen Plainphone)

The words Beaux read for his copy test (round 3 in [copy-test.md](copy-test.md)), now with his fixes applied.
Kept as a record and for later testers, only if Beaux asks for them. It holds the current words only, with the names
changed (Margaret uses the phone, Sam looks after it, Alex is the helper) so the tester doesn't know the real users.

It tests words, not looks. The screen sketches are rough; the real layout and the black-and-white look are in
[design-tokens.md](design-tokens.md). There is no colour on any Plainphone screen.

## How to run it (about 30 minutes)

1. Find someone who has **never** seen Plainphone or these documents, and who is **not** technical. Ideally one
   person for Part 1 (the phone user) and a different person for Part 2 (the trusted person). One person can do
   both if needed: Part 1 first.
2. Give them the part as plain text or a printout. Don't explain anything first. Don't help while they answer.
3. Ask the questions in order. Write down their answers in their own words, especially anything they misread,
   hesitate over, or dislike.
4. Record: first name or initials (or "anonymous"), rough age range, how comfortable they are with phones
   (1–5), date, and who ran the test. Don't record anything else about them.
5. Post the answers on [PLA-12](/PLA/issues/PLA-12) (or reply to the question card there).

## Part 1: the person using the phone

### Questions
1. You want an app for the local bus times. What exactly do you do?
2. The main screen says "Ask Sam to do the monthly phone check" (this line only appears when the check is due). What does that mean? Is something wrong with your phone? What will you do?
3. You tapped a link in a text from a friend and got Screen 6. What happened, in your own words? What would you tap, and why?
4. What happens if you tap "Send to Sam"? Would you feel OK doing that? Why or why not?
5. You're at a hotel and see Screen 7. What does it mean? What would you do? Can you still make a call?
6. After reading Screen 8: who can read your messages? Can Sam see where you are? How do you feel about Sam's role?
7. You see "Plainphone needs attention" on the main screen. Are you worried? What do you do?
8. List every word or sentence you didn't understand or that made you uneasy, and say why.
9. Anything that felt bossy, babyish, or like you were being watched?
Answer each question in 1-4 sentences, numbered.

## Part 2: the trusted person

### Questions
1. Margaret asked for a bus app. Walk through exactly what you'd do, screen by screen.
2. While the Play Store is open, what must you do and not do? Why?
3. When is it OK to give the phone back to Margaret? What if you see "Not ready to hand back – call the helper"?
4. On Screen D, what does "Not reviewed yet – this app may open web pages inside it" mean to you? Would you add the app? Why?
5. On Screen E, how would you decide? Is anything confusing?
6. Name three things Plainphone can't stop, in your own words. Which one worries you most?
7. You see Screen H. Is Margaret's phone OK to use? What do you do next?
8. What does the monthly check involve? Could you do it alone from these instructions?
9. If you turn Plainphone off, what happens to Margaret's photos? Could you turn it back on yourself?
10. Does anything here feel like you're spying on Margaret, or that would make Margaret feel watched?
11. List every word or sentence you didn't understand, found ambiguous, or would change, with a suggested rewording.
Answer each in 1-4 sentences, numbered.

### The screens

```
You are reading the screens of "Plainphone", a simplified phone. Margaret uses the phone. Sam looks after it: Sam adds apps for Margaret, using a PIN, with Margaret's phone in Sam's hands. A "helper" (here also called Alex) set the phone up with a computer.

SCREEN A (Sam opens "For Sam" and enters the PIN). Title: Looking after Margaret's phone
  This is for adding apps and keeping the phone working. You can't see Margaret's messages, photos, or what Margaret does in apps.
  Needs you:
   - 1 request from Margaret
   - Check for updates – due
  Menu:
   Add an app — Opens the Play Store for 10 minutes
   Apps on this phone — Change the order, remove, what each can use
   Requests from Margaret
   Check for updates — Last done: 3 Sep
   Open full phone settings — For 5 minutes, for things Phone settings doesn't have, like the screen lock
   What Plainphone can't stop — Read this before adding apps
   Install a Plainphone update — Only with Alex, when Alex asks
   Change PIN
   Turn Plainphone off — Makes this an ordinary phone
  [ Done – back to Margaret's home screen ]

SCREEN B (request card)
  From Margaret:
  "the bus times app, to check the ferry bus"
  Thursday 8 Oct, 10:40 · Text sent to you ✓
  [ Find it in the Play Store ]  [ Not now ]

SCREEN C (before opening the Play Store). Title: Open the Play Store for 10 minutes
  **Stay with the phone until you tap Done.**
  Keep the phone in your hands. Don't give it to Margaret while the Play Store is open: it can show web pages, so the phone isn't simple for these minutes.
  Search for the app, tap Install, and wait until it says Open. Then go back to the home screen (swipe up from the bottom of the screen). Plainphone checks the phone, then shows Done.
  The Play Store also closes if the screen turns off, a call comes in, or after 10 minutes.
  [ Open the Play Store ]   (then Sam enters the PIN)
  While open, a notification says: "Play Store open · Keep the phone with you · 8 min left · Tap here to finish"

SCREEN D (after installing an app and pressing Home). Title: Add Transit?
  Transit: Bus & Subway Times. Made by Transit Inc.
  Margaret asked for: "the bus times app, to check the ferry bus". This app is Transit, made by Transit Inc. Make sure it's the one Margaret meant.
  Web pages inside this app: Not reviewed yet – this app may open web pages inside it
  The Plainphone team checks apps for this and writes a note for each one. They haven't checked Transit. To ask them to, tell Alex.
  [ Next ]   Don't add (link)
  Next screen. Title: Add an app nobody has checked?
  The Plainphone team hasn't checked whether Transit can show web pages inside it. Some apps can show any website, which would let Margaret reach websites this phone normally keeps out. If you're not sure, wait: ask Alex to get it checked first. You can also add it now and remove it later.
  [ Add anyway ]  Don't add (link)

SCREEN E. Title: What can Transit use?
  Choose together with Margaret. Transit won't ask Margaret about these later, and Margaret can't change them alone. If Margaret wants a change, you change it together, with the phone in your hands: "Apps on this phone" → Transit → "What Transit can use". If you choose "Don't allow", the parts of Transit that need it won't work.
  Location (where the phone is) — Show things near you, or let Transit know where you are   ( Allow ) ( Don't allow )
  Notifications — Show alerts at the top of the screen               ( Allow ) ( Don't allow )
  [ Next ]  (disabled until each one is chosen)
  Then: "Add Transit to Margaret's phone? It will appear on the home screen, below the other apps. Allowed: Notifications. Not allowed: Location." [ Add Transit ] then PIN.

SCREEN F (after leaving the Play Store). Title: Checking the phone…  This takes a few seconds. Keep the phone with you.
  Then either:
  Title: Everything is back in place. The Play Store is closed again. You can hand the phone back to Margaret.  [ Done ]
  or:
  Title: Not ready to hand back – call the helper. Something didn't go back the way it should. Keep the phone with you. Tap Check again once; if this screen comes back, call Alex, your helper. Calls, texts and Emergency still work.  Details for Alex (link)  [ Check again ]

SCREEN G. Title: What Plainphone can't stop
  Plainphone makes this phone much simpler, but it isn't perfect. Here's what it can't stop, so you can decide with Margaret what's OK.
  Web pages inside apps — Some apps show web pages inside themselves, such as help pages, articles or sign-in screens. Plainphone can't control those pages; each app's note tells you what we found.
  Google help and account pages — Some Google screens, like account and help pages, can show web pages. Testing found these on this phone: none so far.
  The Play Store and full settings — When you open the Play Store or full phone settings, the phone is open for those minutes, and their help pages can show the web. That's why you stay with the phone until you tap Done.
  If Plainphone stops working while they're open — If Plainphone breaks while the Play Store or settings are open, they stay open until Alex fixes the phone. Don't hand the phone back until Plainphone says it's ready.
  Apps installed without you — If an app gets installed without you, it can't be opened. But if Plainphone can't hide it, it might still run unseen in the background, for any length of time.
  Erasing the phone with its buttons — Anyone holding the phone can erase it using its buttons (a factory reset). After that, Google asks for your Google account, or the backup person's you chose at setup, before the phone can be used. Plainphone doesn't come back by itself: it has to be set up again, and anything not backed up is lost.
  Wi-Fi with a sign-in page — Wi-Fi that asks you to sign in or accept terms on a page first, like in hotels, hospitals or on ferries, won't work. Mobile data and home Wi-Fi are fine.
  Phone software updates — Android updates can arrive before the Plainphone team has checked them; some security updates install anyway. Until we check, a new update could add a way around Plainphone that we haven't seen.
  A bad Plainphone update — There's no undo for a Plainphone update. If one goes badly wrong, the fix is to wipe the phone and set it up again.
  Emergency alerts — Plainphone doesn't block emergency alerts. We checked that alerts appear on this phone (14 May).
  What Google and apps collect — Plainphone itself sends nothing anywhere. But the phone still runs Google's services, which collect data, and each app follows its own privacy rules.
  [ Done ]

SCREEN H. Title: Phone software changed – review pending
  Android, the phone's own software, updated itself on 2 Nov. The Plainphone team checks each new version for gaps, and hasn't checked this one yet.
  Nothing has changed for Margaret: the same apps, the same limits, and Emergency still works. There's nothing you need to do. Alex has been sent a text and will install a Plainphone update.
  Until then, there's a small chance the update added a gap that lets an app get around Plainphone.
  [ OK ]   What changed (link)

SCREEN I (Check for updates). Title: Check for updates
  You'll open the Play Store's list of updates and update anything that's waiting. Afterwards, Plainphone asks which apps didn't update. About 10 minutes. Updates carry on even after the Play Store closes.
  **Stay with the phone until you tap Done.**
  In the Play Store: tap your Google picture or initial at the top right, then Manage apps & device, then Updates available. Tap Update all if you see it.
  [ Start the check ]   Not now (link)
  Afterwards: "Did everything update?" [ Yes, all up to date ]  Some apps didn't update (link)
  Then: "Confirm the update check. Enter your PIN to confirm you looked at the Play Store's update list. The next check will be due on 8 Nov." [ Confirm ]

SCREEN J (Turn Plainphone off). Title: Turn Plainphone off?
  This makes Margaret's phone an ordinary Android phone again. Every app will open, including web browsers and the Play Store, and the home screen will look different.
  Photos, messages and contacts are kept. Nothing is erased.
  To use Plainphone again later, Alex has to set it up again with a computer.
  Plainphone only turns off this way, with your PIN. A flat battery doesn't turn it off: when the phone starts again, Plainphone is back.
  [ Continue ]  Keep Plainphone on (link)
  Then: "Are you sure? Plainphone will be turned off on Margaret's phone." [ Yes, turn it off ]  then PIN
  Then: "Last check. Once it's off, only Alex can turn it back on, using a computer. Keep the phone on and plugged in if you can. It takes a minute or two." [ Turn off now ]

SCREEN K (wrong PIN). Title: Too many tries
  Wait 2 minutes before trying again. You can use the recovery code now instead (the long code you wrote down when Plainphone was set up).
  Calls, texts and Emergency still work.
  [ Use recovery code ]  OK (link)
```
